Research

Our program sits at the intersection of magnetism, condensed-matter theory, and device physics. We ask how electrical currents, voltages, heat, and light can write, read, and couple to spin—then turn those mechanisms into memories, sensors, and unconventional computers.

Spin–orbit torque and topological spintronics

Spin–orbit interaction in heavy metals and topological insulators provides an electrical knob on magnetic order. The group has contributed to field-free switching of perpendicular magnets, giant spin–orbit torque in magnetically doped topological insulators, and voltage control of torque efficiency—mechanisms for energy-efficient magnetic memory and logic.

Skyrmions and chiral textures

Magnetic skyrmions are particle-like whirls of magnetization that can store and transport information. We have contributed to current-induced creation and motion of room-temperature skyrmion bubbles, electric-field guiding of skyrmions, and—more recently—proposals to braid Majorana modes with skyrmion textures, as well as noncollinear order and 90° domain walls in twisted XY magnets.

Quantum spin defects and magnon hybrids

Optically addressable defects—nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond and boron vacancies in hexagonal boron nitride—let us probe magnons, map strain, and sense electric fields. An NSF CAREER award supports spin–magnon hybrid quantum devices: scalable local control of defect qubits and new probes of chiral condensed-matter phenomena. Ongoing work maps sub-terahertz spin–lattice relaxation of hBN boron vacancies, including ab initio acoustic-phonon theory.

Chiral antiferromagnets and probabilistic computing

Chiral antiferromagnets host octupole order that fluctuates on picosecond scales and can be tuned electrically. We use that dynamics as a materials platform for robust, ultra-fast probabilistic bits (p-bits), synaptic plasticity, and octupole-driven spin-transfer torque in all-antiferromagnetic tunnel junctions—hardware for stochastic and neuromorphic computing.

Quantum metamaterials and computational magnetism

Complementing analytics and experiment, we build high-performance models: Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert–Slonczewski dynamics for three-sublattice Mn3Pt-type systems, phase-field ferroelectric solvers, and micromagnetics of moiré magnets. Machine-learning surrogates (PINNs, neural operators) help discover models from spintronic data.

Bio-devices and next-generation information processing

Related interests include spintronic probes for biological systems and hybrid quantum–classical algorithms. Across these efforts, spin is used as a compact, low-energy information carrier.
